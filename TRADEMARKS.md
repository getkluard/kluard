## You may, without asking

- Say that you use, run, host, teach or write about Kluard, and use the name for that purpose ("we monitor our Nextcloud with Kluard").
- Say that your product works with, imports from or integrates with Kluard ("works with Kluard"), if it is true.
- Say that you run a service based on the software ("Powered by Kluard", "Nextcloud hosting, monitored with Kluard"), as long as
  you do not suggest that we run it or endorse it.
- Redistribute **unmodified** releases of Kluard under the name Kluard, with the licence files and notices left in place.
- Use the name in articles, talks, videos, community meetups and user groups about Kluard, if it is clear that you are not us.
- Link to kluard.com and github.com/getkluard/kluard, and show the logo to link to them, unchanged and without a frame or effect.

## You may not, without our written permission

- Use a Mark, or a name that looks or sounds like one, as the name of your own product, company, service, domain name, app,
  package, repository, social media account or event (for example "Kluard Cloud", "Kluard Pro", "kluard-hosting.com").
- Use a Mark for a **modified version** of the software. If you change the code and distribute it, or offer it as a service, give
  it a different name and say that it is based on Kluard. Small patches that you apply for yourself, or to be sent back to us, are fine.
- Say or suggest that we made, approved, certified or support your product, fork or service.
- Use the names "Kluard Enterprise" or "Kluard Operator" for anything that is not the edition we license under those names.
- Change, redraw, animate or combine the logo with other marks, or use it as part of your own logo.
- Use the Marks in a way that is misleading, defamatory or illegal, or that harms our reputation.

## Forks and distributions

A fork is welcome under the Apache licence. Pick a name of your own, keep the Apache notices, and say that your project is
"based on Kluard". Linux distributions and package maintainers may use the name Kluard for packages that contain our
unmodified releases; if you must patch something to fit your distribution, tell us and keep the patch small and public.

## The Enterprise Edition

The Enterprise Edition (the `ee/` directory and the files named `*_ee.go`) is not open source and is covered by
[ee/LICENSE](ee/LICENSE). Nothing in this policy gives you a right to use it in production without a licence.
